Wireshark: Normal vs. Suspicious Traffic

Traffic patterns, red flags, and what deserves a closer look.

Jul 21, 2026 6 min read Networking

Not every unusual packet is malicious. Context, baselines, and repetition matter when deciding whether traffic deserves further investigation.

Begin with a baseline

Before looking for suspicious behaviour, it helps to understand what normal traffic looks like in the environment. Common DNS, HTTPS, DHCP, and local network traffic provide useful reference points.

Source:      192.168.1.10
Destination: 142.250.74.14
Protocol:    TCP
Port:        443
State:       ESTABLISHED

Patterns worth investigating

  • Repeated outbound connections at regular intervals.
  • Unexpected protocols or destination ports.
  • DNS requests for unusual or newly observed domains.
  • Large transfers that do not match expected activity.

Context changes everything

An unfamiliar destination may belong to a legitimate cloud service. A repeated connection could be a normal software update check. The goal is not to label traffic immediately, but to ask better questions.
